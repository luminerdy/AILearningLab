AI Learning Lab Continued Learning Path

The three-hour workshop is the starting point. These follow-on labs give students further practice and can become additional class sessions. The durations below are planning estimates, not measured completion times. Work through the labs that help your goals; the full progression is not a prerequisite chain.

Save evidence for every lab

Keep a small learning record containing your goal and audience, criteria, the information you supplied, requests and responses, expected and actual check results, a revision, and a reflection. Explain what you did yourself and what AI contributed. Use the workbook's tables or a document of your own.

Lab 1 Adapt a message for two audiences

Practice: ASK and COLLABORATE. Estimated time: 30–45 minutes.

Use the workshop's fictional event facts. Create one announcement for a new student and another for a club adviser. Before asking AI, define what each reader needs and set a length limit. Check both outputs for preserved facts and audience fit. Ask a peer to explain each message's next action. Revise one message based on that evidence.

Deliver: Two messages, checks, and a short explanation of how the audience changed your direction.

Lab 2 Build a study helper from supplied notes

Practice: ASK, COLLABORATE, and evidence-based checking. Estimated time: 45–60 minutes.

Use this fictional learning passage: “A model town has 120 homes. Forty homes collect rainwater. Each collecting home stores 50 liters per week. The town uses this collected water for gardens. The passage does not state how much water the gardens need.”

Ask AI for four practice questions: one recall question, one fraction question, one multiplication question, and one question about missing information. Request an answer key separately. Solve the questions yourself and check every answer against the passage. If an answer introduces unsupported assumptions, revise it and check again.

Instructor reference: 40 homes; 40/120 = 1/3; 40 × 50 = 2,000 liters per week; whether collection meets garden demand cannot be determined from the passage.

Deliver: Questions, corrected answers, your calculations, and a documented revision. Follow your instructor's rules when adapting this to actual coursework.

Lab 3 Give AI a project brief

Practice: ORIENT through useful context. Estimated time: 45–60 minutes.

Choose a fictional school activity. Write a brief containing its purpose, audience, known facts, constraints, tone, and unanswered questions. Label fictional facts you invent before giving them to AI. Ask for a short plan using the brief. Compare the plan with every constraint and mark assumptions separately from supplied facts. Revise the brief where ambiguity caused a problem, then try again.

Deliver: The brief, plan, constraint checks, and an explanation of which context mattered. Repository instructions such as AGENTS.md are a later implementation of this practice; this lab needs only a document.

Lab 4 Turn your process into a reusable procedure

Practice: TEACH through captured know-how. Estimated time: 45–60 minutes.

Turn a successful earlier exercise into a procedure another student can follow. Include needed inputs, steps, output requirements, checks, and what to do if a check fails. Give the procedure to a partner with a different fictional event brief. Observe where they need clarification and improve the procedure.

Deliver: A reusable procedure, a partner's trial, and a revision. A procedure is the learning foundation for a skill; no installed skill or coding tool is required.

Lab 5 Complete a small project

Practice: Combine ASK, COLLABORATE, and ORIENT. Estimated time: 90–120 minutes.

Create a small information kit for a fictional club event: a project brief, announcement, FAQ, and preparation checklist. Define how the artifacts must agree before creating them. Check cross-document consistency, unsupported details, completeness, and reader understanding. Invite another student to review the kit. Make an evidence-driven revision and describe remaining limitations.

Deliver: The kit and a learning record showing at least two full Define → Direct → Check → Adjust cycles. The goal is a coherent, checked project, rather than a large volume of output.

Later extensions

ENABLE, DELEGATE, and ORCHESTRATE can be added once useful tasks, access arrangements, and appropriate checks are defined. Future activities might use a spreadsheet to verify a result, give an agent a bounded assignment, or design a workflow with human review points. These modules are not yet developed exercises and do not require students to install tools now.

A possible multi-day course

Day Work Evidence
1 Three-hour introductory workshop Completed workbook and exit ticket
2 Labs 1 and 2 with discussion and revision Audience comparison and checked study helper
3 Labs 3 and 4 with partner trials Context brief and tested procedure
4 Lab 5 with review and presentations Checked project and learning record

Schedule breaks, recaps, and support around the lab estimates. Students may also complete these labs independently after the workshop. Reflect after each lab on what is comfortable and repeatable, what you have tried, and what would help next.
