LLM Basics Before You Begin

Spend about 15 minutes on these ideas before the workshop exercises. You do not need to understand model architecture to use AI thoughtfully. Learn enough terminology to describe what you are doing and recognize why checking matters.

What is an LLM

LLM means large language model. A model learns patterns in language during training. For text generation, it uses those learned patterns and the available context to predict possible next pieces of text, called tokens. It generates a continuation one token at a time.

“Predicts the next word” is a useful starting explanation. “Predicts the next token” is more accurate. This describes how text is generated; models also receive additional training to follow instructions and produce useful responses. How language models are developed.

A token is a piece of text

A token can be a whole word, part of a word, punctuation, or another small piece of text. Token boundaries depend on the model's tokenizer, which divides text into these units. A token count is not a word count. Understanding tokens.

For this workshop, you only need to remember that the model processes text in pieces. There is no need to memorize token counts or learn a tokenizer.

Context changes the continuation

Try completing this sentence:

I put on my shoes because I was going to…

Several endings could fit: school, the park, or a friend's house. Now add context:

It was the first day of classes. I put on my shoes because I was going to…

The extra information changes which continuation seems plausible. This is an analogy for prediction, rather than a demonstration of exact token boundaries or model probabilities.

For your AI task, context includes the relevant instructions, conversation, and information supplied to the model. Giving it your audience, goal, facts, and constraints helps direct its response. Useful context still needs checking.

Training and a conversation are different

Training develops the model's learned patterns. Generating a response, also called inference, uses the model with the available input. Supplying a fact in a conversation can help it answer that task; it does not by itself retrain the underlying model.

Do not assume the model has every fact about your situation. Supply the information needed for the task, and check that the response uses it correctly.

What is a hallucination

An AI hallucination is a plausible-sounding but false statement generated by a model. Examples include an invented date, a quotation someone never said, or a reference that does not exist. Fluent text can sound certain even when it is wrong. Training for plausible continuations does not guarantee factual accuracy. Why language models hallucinate.

An invented detail can also be unsupported by the information you supplied. Even when you cannot establish whether it is false, you should flag it for checking. Creative invention is useful when you asked for fiction; presenting an invented detail as a real fact is a problem.

Spot the unsupported detail

Use these fictional facts:

  • The club meets Thursday in Room 204.
  • Beginners are welcome.
  • Sign up with the club adviser by Wednesday.

Here is a deliberately written practice response, not a captured AI answer:

Join us Friday in Room 204. Beginners are welcome. Register at club-example.school to meet our guest speaker.

With a partner, identify what is supported, contradicted, or unsupported. Then write a request that would correct the answer without filling gaps by guessing.

Check your findings: Room 204 and the beginner welcome match the facts. Friday contradicts Thursday. The signup website and guest speaker are unsupported. The response also omits the supplied signup method and deadline. A useful revision should use Thursday, retain the supported facts, include signup with the adviser by Wednesday, and remove unsupported additions.

Terms you will use

Term Meaning in this workshop
AI Artificial intelligence; the broad category of tools we are exploring
LLM A large language model that learns language patterns and generates text
Token A piece of text processed by the model
Prompt The request or instructions you give AI
Context Information and instructions available for the response
Response The output returned to you
Hallucination A plausible-sounding false statement generated by AI
Evidence Something you can inspect or test to support a conclusion

Bring this into the learning loop

  • Define: Decide what you want and how you will recognize a useful result.
  • Direct: Supply a clear request and relevant context. Ask AI to identify missing information rather than invent it.
  • Check: Compare claims with supplied facts or dependable sources. Test requirements yourself. A confident tone, repeated answer, or convincing explanation is not proof.
  • Adjust: Correct a specific problem and check the revised result again.

Asking AI to avoid guessing is useful direction, but it is not a guarantee. Keep asking “What would convince me this is right?”

Before moving on, explain to a partner why a token is not always a word and why a fluent answer still needs evidence.
